How every session works

Open Claude and say AWS Day 1 (or whatever day you're on). Claude tracks where you are, what you got wrong, and what to review.

  1. Learn (25 to 40 min). Claude teaches the day's topic in plain English, compared to stuff you already know (Cloudflare, your sites). Then you watch the matching part of the free course.
  2. Build it yourself (30 to 45 min). You type every command and click every button. Claude explains, you don't paste.
  3. Break it (10 min). Claude has you break something on purpose and figure out why from the error.
  4. Prove it (10 to 15 min). 10 exam-style questions plus 3 interview questions you answer out loud, in your own words. Anything you miss goes on your review list.

Saturdays: review the week, redo one lab from a blank screen with no help, and do a mock interview.

Interview basics they'll think are "simple"

The exam doesn't test all of these, but junior interviews do. Each one is built into the days above, and Claude will drill you on them until you can explain every one out loud.

  • What happens when you type a website into your browser, step by step
  • DNS record types: A, AAAA, CNAME, MX, TXT, NS
  • HTTP status codes: 200, 301, 302, 400, 401, 403, 404, 500, 502, 503, 504
  • Common ports: 22 SSH, 80 HTTP, 443 HTTPS, 3306 MySQL, 5432 Postgres, 3389 RDP
  • TCP vs UDP
  • Public vs private IP addresses, what NAT does
  • CIDR: what a /16, /24, /32 mean and how many addresses each has
  • Linux: ls, cd, cat, grep, chmod, chown, ps, top, df, du, systemctl, journalctl, curl, ssh
  • "The website is down. How do you figure out why?"
  • Security group vs network ACL
  • Scaling up vs scaling out
  • High availability vs fault tolerance vs disaster recovery
  • What a load balancer does and why you'd use one
  • IAM user vs role, and why you never put access keys in code
  • Encryption at rest vs in transit
  • Backups vs replicas
  • SQL vs NoSQL, and when you'd pick each
  • RPO vs RTO
  • What a container is vs a virtual machine
  • Serverless: what it is and when it's a bad idea
  • How you'd cut a company's AWS bill
  • Git basics: commit, branch, pull request, merge
  • Tell me about a time something broke and you fixed it (your real client stories)
  • Walk me through a project you built (your AWS project + Film Timeline)

Project checklist (one deep project beats five tutorials)

The project has to:

  1. Cost: it costs real money and you brought the cost down (or can explain how you thought about it).
  2. Permissions: locked down. Least privilege, nothing sensitive open to the internet.
  3. Monitoring: logs and alarms set up, so you'd know if it broke.
  4. Decisions: you made the design choices yourself and can explain why for each one.
  5. Incident write-up: when something broke, you wrote what happened, why, and what you changed.
  6. Built as code: Terraform in a public GitHub repo, not clicked together in the console.

Presenting it:

  • Architecture diagram at the TOP of the README.
  • A short recorded walkthrough of it running.
  • On the resume: clear project name plus the business problem it solves. Save the list of AWS services for the interview.

Applying:

  • Build one long master resume (every project, client job, and skill). Never send it.
  • For each job you actually want, copy it and cut it down to fit that posting.
  • 5 to 10 tailored applications a week beats 50 generic ones.
